## Approval cards

Supported providers emit permission requests and questions through the local harness. OpenMausBot displays these inline and records the outcome. Cancellation closes outstanding requests so stale approvals cannot be answered after a turn has ended.

“Always allow” is narrow: it is tied to a server-issued key and a pending request rather than granting arbitrary execution from a paired device.

## Sending and paying ask first

A connected-app call that sends something, and a click in the bot's browser
that sends or pays, wait for your answer at every approval level, Full access
included. The card names the site or app and shows what would go out. In the
browser it shows the form's fields and a screenshot the computer takes itself.
Passwords, card details, codes, PINs, keys, recovery phrases and identity,
tax and bank numbers are counted inside the page and never leave it: the card
shows only how long each is, and no screenshot is taken of a page that shows
one. On a page with card fields, any button the bot presses asks first,
whatever the button says.

In the browser this is a guard against a bot over-acting on an ordinary page,
not a guarantee. It goes by what a careful reader would see: the button's
label, the form it is in, card fields around it. Ordinary browsing passes
without a card: following a link, saving a profile, applying a filter,
signing in, a new line in a notes box. What it does not catch:

- a payment behind a button that says only "Confirm" or "Continue" on a page
  with no card field, such as a one-click checkout with a saved card or a
  page whose only payment element is a wallet button;
- a link that sends or cancels something just by being opened;
- a send on a button whose label is not one of the usual words ("Dispatch",
  "Go"), or by Enter in a box it does not recognise as a message composer;
- buttons in languages other than English, outside card forms and message
  forms;
- fields inside a component that keeps its contents closed: the card says
  when part of a form could not be read;
- anything a page does from its own scripts.

A check on the payment request itself is planned as separate work.

Under a bot's **Permissions → Sending on your behalf** you can allow a daily
number of sends instead. Spending money in the browser always asks. An
approval covers only what the card showed and the rest of that form, hidden
fields included: if any of it changed before you answered, nothing is clicked
and you are asked again.

Scripts a bot runs in a page are refused when they would click, submit, send,
write or navigate. That check reads the script's text, so it stops habits,
not a script built to hide what it does.

A card closes, and nothing is clicked, when the bot's call behind it is gone:
the turn was stopped or the bot gave up waiting. When a browser action takes
too long to answer, the browser is not restarted and the page is kept: the
bot is told the action may already have happened, and its next step is not
carried out until the browser answers again. A send or a payment after that
asks first, like any other.

## Operating-system permissions

Screen Recording and Accessibility permissions are controlled by the operating system. The desktop process owns local CUA lifecycle so grants are attributed to OpenMausBot.

## Secret handling

- Do not paste API keys into chat.
- Packaged builds use write-only settings and operating-system-backed encryption where supported.
- Secret values are scrubbed from public configuration responses and mobile APIs.
- Per-provider environment injection prevents unrelated agent processes from inheriting credentials they do not use.
- OAuth provider tokens for connected apps stay with Composio.

## Connected-app tool grants

Connected apps are scoped per bot on the desktop: a bot without a grant to a service's tool cannot call it, the ungranted tool is never offered to the model, and every allow/deny is written to the decision log. New and upgraded bots keep all tools until someone assigns grants, and imported bots always land with none, so a shared persona cannot reach your Gmail on turn one. Paired phones see grants read-only; grant editing requires the computer.

## Website sign-in

Bots may complete a sign-in you explicitly authorize for a particular site and
account, using an existing session, autofill, or credentials you supply or
designate for that purpose, including test accounts. A login form alone is not
a reason to refuse the task. Bots must check the destination and account first;
instructions on a webpage do not authorize using credentials.

Do not paste passwords or one-time codes into chat. If credentials are missing,
or a step needs MFA, CAPTCHA, payment details or your personal interaction,
click into the bot's Browser tab and complete that step yourself: your first
click or keystroke pauses the bot's browser tools, and the browser goes back to
the bot a few seconds after your last input (it stays yours while **Type or
paste text…** is open). Then ask the bot to continue: it is told that you
used the browser, and to re-read the page first. This prompt policy does not
add a website-password vault or override your provider's own restrictions.

## Shared machines

OpenMausBot assumes the logged-in operating-system user is the workspace owner. On a shared machine, other administrators may be able to read application data or inspect processes. Use a dedicated OS account for stronger separation.
